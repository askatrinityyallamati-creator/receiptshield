AI-Based Receipt Fraud & Tampering Detection System

Does that receipt actually add up?

ReceiptShield reads a digital receipt or bill and checks whether it may have been altered — and whether its information is mathematically and logically consistent. It reads the items, quantities, prices, tax, discount and total with OCR, proves the arithmetic, then studies the image itself for edited text, unusual fonts, spacing and misalignment.

    How the seven stages work
    
        
    ReceiptShield combines OCR, mathematical validation and image-based tampering detection instead of checking only the visual appearance of a receipt.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    01
    Receipt Upload
    
          
    The image is normalised, upscaled for legibility and kept entirely in the browser. EXIF and embedded-editor markers are inspected but never uploaded.
    
        
    02
    OCR Extraction
    
          
    A Tesseract engine runs in a web worker and returns raw text plus a word-level geometry table: bounding boxes, baselines and per-word confidence.
    
        
    03
    Item / Price / Tax Detection
    
          
    Lines are classified into items, modifiers, discounts, taxes and totals. Quantities, unit prices, percentages and currency amounts are parsed into numbers.
    
        
    04
    Mathematical Validation
    
          
    Every equation the receipt asserts gets recomputed: qty × unit against line totals, items against the subtotal, subtotal − discount + tax against the grand total.
    
        
    05
    Visual Tampering Analysis
    
          
    Error-level analysis exposes re-compression residue from pasted regions, tone mapping spots patches whose background does not match, and typography metrics catch odd font sizes, baselines and spacing.
    
        
    06
    Suspicious Information Detection
    
          
    Logic and statistics check the rest: amount columns that stop aligning, duplicate lines, missing tax, implausible rates, round-number edits and low-confidence clusters.
    
        
    07
    Fraud Risk Report
    
          
    Findings are weighted by severity into a 0–100 risk score, then presented with the evidence behind each one and an exportable JSON payload.
    
      
    

      
    
        
    Innovation
    
        
    It combines OCR, mathematical validation and image-based tampering detection instead of checking only the visual appearance of a receipt, making it useful for expense verification and fraud detection.
    
      
    
    
    
  
    

  
  
    
    
    
      
    What this can and cannot claim
    
      
    
        
    
          
    It is evidence, not proof
    
          
    ReceiptShield is a heuristic triage tool. A flagged receipt is a reason to look closer, not a finding of fraud. Compression, scanning, glare and phone cameras all create artefacts that resemble tampering.
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Where it is strongest
    
          
    Edited amounts, quantities or totals that break the arithmetic; text pasted from another font; patches whose background tone or noise does not match; and files that were re-saved by an image editor.
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Where it is blind
    
          
    A forger who re-types the whole receipt in one font, keeps the arithmetic consistent and exports a clean PNG leaves little trace. Very low-resolution or heavily compressed receipts defeat the pixel-level stages.
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Privacy
    
          
    Everything runs client-side. The receipt image is read locally through a canvas, the OCR engine is fetched from a CDN on first use, and nothing is transmitted to or stored on a server.
